Editorial Policy

How articles are researched, written, reviewed, updated, and corrected.

Last updated: October 1, 2026

Our Editorial Purpose

The Great Library of Philosophy is an educational reference project dedicated to explaining philosophers, philosophical traditions, concepts, arguments, works, and the history of philosophy in a clear and accessible way. Our editorial approach is intended to help readers distinguish established historical information from philosophical interpretation, scholarly disagreement, and later criticism.

We aim to produce articles that are accurate, understandable, appropriately sourced, and useful to both general readers and students. Articles are written to explain philosophical ideas rather than to promote a particular philosophical, political, religious, or ideological position.

Research and Sources

Articles are developed using reliable primary and secondary sources appropriate to the subject. Where possible, we consult a philosopher's own works alongside established scholarly and reference materials.

  • Primary sources: philosophical works, letters, lectures, manuscripts, and other writings attributed to the thinker being discussed.
  • Scholarly sources: academic books, university-press publications, peer-reviewed scholarship, and other established academic research.
  • Reference works: reputable academic encyclopedias and reference resources, including the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy and the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy.
  • Institutional sources: university, library, museum, archive, and other authoritative institutional resources where relevant.

Sources are selected according to the subject and the type of claim being made. Biographical facts, dates, titles of works, historical events, quotations, and other factual information are checked against reliable references where reasonably possible.

Articles may include a Sources & Further Reading section to help readers investigate a subject beyond the article itself. Sources listed for further reading are intended as research resources and do not necessarily mean that every interpretation in an article is taken directly from a particular source.

Facts, Interpretation, and Scholarly Disagreement

Philosophy frequently involves competing interpretations. An article may therefore distinguish between historical facts, a philosopher's stated position, later scholarly interpretations, and criticisms of that position.

When there is significant scholarly disagreement, we aim to identify the disagreement rather than present a contested interpretation as an undisputed fact. Where an interpretation is commonly associated with a thinker but is debated by scholars, the wording should make that distinction clear.

We also distinguish between what a philosopher actually argued and what later writers, commentators, or popular accounts have attributed to them. Quotations and interpretations should not be presented as the philosopher's own words or views unless the available evidence supports that attribution.

Accuracy and Editorial Standards

Accuracy is a central editorial priority. We make reasonable efforts to verify factual claims before publication and to correct information when a credible error is identified.

  • We avoid presenting uncertain claims as established facts.
  • We identify major scholarly disagreements when they are relevant to understanding a subject.
  • We avoid fabricated quotations, unsupported biographical claims, and misleading attribution.
  • We distinguish a philosopher's own position from later commentary about that position.
  • We aim to explain philosophical arguments in their historical and intellectual context rather than reducing them to isolated quotations or slogans.

Because philosophical scholarship is continually developing, no reference article should be understood as permanently complete. Readers are encouraged to consult the cited sources and further scholarship for deeper or specialized study.

Use of AI Tools

AI-assisted tools may be used during parts of the editorial process, such as brainstorming, organizing material, improving wording, or identifying areas that require further research. AI-generated information is not treated as a substitute for source verification.

Published material is reviewed and edited by a human author before publication. Factual claims, quotations, dates, references, and other important information should be checked against appropriate sources rather than accepted solely because an AI system produced them.

The use of an AI-assisted tool does not transfer editorial responsibility to the tool or its provider. The site's human author remains responsible for the final published material.

Updates and Review

Articles may be updated when factual errors are identified, when additional reliable information becomes available, when scholarship materially changes the understanding of a subject, or when an article can be made clearer and more useful to readers.

Articles display a Last updated date to indicate when the page was most recently substantially reviewed or revised. Minor changes such as spelling, punctuation, formatting, broken-link corrections, or other non-substantive edits may not result in a change to the displayed date.

Updates do not necessarily mean that an article's overall interpretation has changed. Where a substantial correction or revision materially affects the substance of an article, the relevant section may be revised to reflect the change.

Corrections

We welcome responsible corrections from readers, researchers, students, and other knowledgeable individuals. If you identify a factual error, incorrect attribution, misleading statement, broken source, inaccurate quotation, or other significant problem, please contact us through the contact page.

When reporting an error, please provide the page URL, identify the relevant passage, explain the problem, and provide a reliable source supporting the proposed correction where possible.

Reported corrections are reviewed against reliable evidence. Confirmed errors may be corrected directly on the page. Significant corrections may also be documented within the article when doing so helps readers understand the nature of the change.

A reader's disagreement with an interpretation does not necessarily constitute a factual error. Where the disagreement reflects a genuine scholarly debate, the article may instead be revised to represent the competing interpretations more accurately.

Quotations and Attribution

Quotations are used selectively and should be attributed to their original author or appropriate source. We avoid presenting paraphrases, summaries, or later interpretations as direct quotations.

Where a quotation has been translated from another language, the source and translation context should be identified where relevant. Readers should consult the cited primary or scholarly source when exact wording or translation is important to their research.

Images and Visual Material

Images used on the site are selected for their relevance to the subject and are used in accordance with the applicable copyright, licensing, or public-domain requirements to the extent reasonably possible.

Where an image requires attribution, the relevant creator, source, or license information should be provided in the appropriate location. Images are not intended to imply that a depicted person, institution, publisher, or rights holder endorses The Great Library of Philosophy.

Advertising, Affiliate Links, and Editorial Independence

Editorial content is intended to remain independent of commercial interests. If advertising, sponsorships, affiliate links, or other commercial relationships are introduced, they should be clearly identifiable to readers.

Commercial relationships should not determine whether a philosopher, book, school of thought, or philosophical position is included or how an editorial article describes it.

Affiliate links, where used, may provide a commission to the site when a reader makes a qualifying purchase. Such links do not change the price paid by the reader unless the relevant retailer's terms state otherwise.

Authorship and Editorial Responsibility

Articles published by The Great Library of Philosophy are produced under the editorial responsibility of the site's author. The author is responsible for reviewing and editing published material and for addressing credible reports of factual errors.

The site is an independent educational project and is not an official publication of the philosophers, universities, publishers, encyclopedias, archives, or other organizations mentioned or cited on its pages.

Copyright and Reuse

Original text and original design elements created for The Great Library of Philosophy are protected by applicable copyright law unless otherwise stated.

Third-party material, including quotations and images, may be subject to separate copyright or licensing conditions. Readers should consult the relevant source or license before reproducing such material.

For additional information about permitted use of site material, please see the Terms of Use.

Questions and Editorial Feedback

If you have identified a factual error, attribution problem, broken source, or other editorial concern, please contact us through the contact page. Providing the relevant page, passage, and supporting source will help us investigate the issue more efficiently.

We value constructive feedback that helps make the library more accurate, transparent, and useful for readers studying philosophy.
