How this page treats quotes

This page treats quotations attributed to Chanakya with caution because many sayings circulating online are presented under his name without a reliable source, while others are modern paraphrases, translations, or sayings drawn from the broader Chanakya Niti tradition. We therefore distinguish between a quotation that can be traced to a specific textual source and a popular saying that is merely attributed to Chanakya. Where possible, quotations are accompanied by the name of the relevant work, book or chapter, verse reference, and translation or edition so that readers can check the wording for themselves. Because Sanskrit texts can vary between manuscripts, editions, and translations, small differences in wording or numbering do not necessarily indicate that a quotation is false, but an exact attribution should not be claimed when its textual source cannot be established. For quotations whose provenance remains uncertain, this page identifies them as attributed or unverified rather than presenting them as securely documented words of the historical Chanakya.

Traditional maxims attributed to Chanakya

Idea (paraphrased) Status

These examples illustrate an important distinction on a page of quotations attributed to Chanakya: a familiar saying may belong to the traditional Chanakya Niti corpus without the exact wording found on modern quotation websites being an original quotation from the historical Chanakya. The entries above are therefore presented as paraphrased ideas rather than as exact quotations. Where a saying can be traced to a particular Sanskrit verse, readers should check the cited edition and translation for its original wording, context, and reference number. Where the source cannot be established with sufficient confidence, it is better to describe the saying as traditionally attributed to Chanakya than to present it as a verified quotation. For an explanation of the text traditionally known as Chanakya Niti, see Chanakya Niti explained.

Popular lines we could not trace

Many quotations circulate online under Chanakya's name without a clear reference to a Sanskrit text, reliable edition, or identifiable verse. Some may be modern paraphrases of ideas found in traditional Niti literature, while others may have been created or altered much more recently. The following examples are widely repeated with Chanakya's name, but we have not been able to trace the wording as given to the Arthashastra or to a standard textual recension of Chanakya Niti:

  • “A man is great by deeds, not by birth.” This saying is frequently presented as a quotation from Chanakya, but the popular English wording should not be treated as a verified quotation without a specific Sanskrit source and verse reference. Similar ideas about conduct, character, and merit may occur in Indian ethical literature, but similarity of an idea does not establish the authenticity of this particular wording.
  • The advice to ask three questions before starting any work. Versions of this saying commonly circulate online as Chanakya's advice about asking what will be gained, why a task is being undertaken, and whether it will succeed. Although the principle of considering consequences before acting is compatible with the broader tradition of practical wisdom associated with Chanakya, this popular formulation should not be presented as a verified quotation unless a traceable textual source is provided.
  • The advice not to fear failure once a task has been started. Modern quotation collections often attribute motivational statements of this kind to Chanakya, sometimes with substantially different English wording. Such statements may express a general idea of persistence or determination, but a motivational sentiment by itself is not evidence that the sentence appeared in a Sanskrit work attributed to Chanakya. Without a source reference, it is safer to identify the saying as attributed rather than verified.
  • Modern motivational statements about success and determination. Numerous short lines about success, hard work, failure, ambition, and never giving up are attributed to Chanakya on social-media posts and quotation websites. Their wording often sounds distinctly modern and differs considerably between websites. Unless such a statement can be connected to a specific Sanskrit verse or reliable translation, it should not be presented as a direct quotation from Chanakya.
  • Modern sayings about choosing friends and identifying enemies. Chanakya is genuinely associated with teachings about friendship, trust, enemies, and prudent relationships, which makes these subjects especially easy to misattribute. A modern sentence expressing a plausible Chanakyan idea is not automatically an authentic quotation. The exact wording, source, and textual reference still need to be established before it can be treated as a documented quotation.
  • Short “Chanakya quotes” created from expanded translations. Some quotations are not completely invented but appear to be condensed or heavily paraphrased versions of longer Sanskrit passages. Once a translator's explanation is shortened into a single memorable English sentence, it can begin circulating as though Chanakya originally wrote those exact words. Such material should be described as a paraphrase or interpretation unless the original verse and translation can be identified.
  • Quotes presented without any book, chapter, verse, or edition. A quotation that appears repeatedly online is not necessarily a verified quotation. If websites simply attribute a sentence to “Chanakya” without identifying the Sanskrit source, textual location, translation, or edition, there is no reliable way for a reader to independently check the attribution. Absence of a citation does not by itself prove that a saying is fabricated, but it is a strong reason to label the attribution cautiously.

These examples should therefore be treated as unverified or cautiously attributed sayings, not automatically as authentic words of Chanakya. A saying may express an idea that resembles Chanakya Niti or another Sanskrit maxim tradition and still have an uncertain textual history. For that reason, this page distinguishes between verified quotations, where a saying can be connected to a traceable source, and popular attributions, where the wording circulates under Chanakya's name but its original source cannot currently be established with sufficient confidence. When no reliable citation is available, formulations such as “traditionally attributed to Chanakya” or “widely attributed to Chanakya” are more accurate than presenting the saying as a confirmed quotation.

How to check a Chanakya quote

The safest way to verify a quotation attributed to Chanakya is to trace it back to a specific Sanskrit text rather than relying on websites, social media posts, or quote collections. A genuine attribution should ideally identify the work, book or chapter, verse or section number, and the translation or edition being used. The following checks can help separate a traceable quotation from a modern paraphrase or an unsupported attribution.

  1. Look for a precise source. A reliable quotation should give more than simply “Chanakya said”. Look for the name of the work, book or chapter, and verse or section number. If a website provides only Chanakya's name without any textual reference, treat the quotation as unverified until a source can be found.
  2. Check the original work and a reliable translation. For quotations from the Arthashastra, compare the cited passage with a scholarly or established edition or translation. A public-domain English translation such as R. Shamasastry's 1915 translation can be useful for locating passages, while modern scholarly translations such as R. P. Kangle's edition or Patrick Olivelle's work can help with wording, interpretation, and textual context. Differences between translations do not automatically mean that a quotation is false.
  3. Make sure the source is actually the right text. Chanakya is traditionally associated with both the Arthashastra and the Chanakya Niti tradition, but these should not be treated as the same work. A saying found in a Niti collection should be identified as belonging to that textual tradition rather than automatically being presented as a passage from the Arthashastra.
  4. Compare the wording with the cited passage. Many popular quotations are shortened translations, modern paraphrases, or polished motivational versions of a much longer passage. If the idea can be located in the source but the exact wording cannot, describe it as a paraphrase rather than putting the modern wording in quotation marks as though it were the original text.
  5. Check the edition and translation. Sanskrit works can have differences in manuscript transmission, editions, verse numbering, and translation. Recording the translator, edition, and publication details makes a quotation much easier for another reader to verify and helps prevent a citation from being copied incorrectly from one website to another.
  6. If no reliable source can be found, qualify the attribution. Do not turn repeated internet attribution into proof of authenticity. When a saying cannot be traced to a specific textual passage, use wording such as “traditionally attributed to Chanakya” or “widely attributed to Chanakya” rather than presenting it as a securely verified quotation.

In short, the name “Chanakya” is not enough to verify a quote. The strongest evidence is a traceable passage in the relevant text, supported by a reliable edition or translation. This approach also helps distinguish an authentic translated passage from a later paraphrase, popular maxim, or modern quotation that has simply acquired Chanakya's name online.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most famous Chanakya quote?

Several lines are widely shared, but many are unverified. The idea that a king's happiness lies in his subjects' happiness is a well-sourced Arthashastra passage.

Are Chanakya quotes on social media authentic?

Many are not traceable to the Arthashastra or standard Chanakya Niti editions. Treat them as “attributed” unless a source is given.

Where can I find real Chanakya quotes?

In the Arthashastra, using the Shamasastry, Kangle, Rangarajan or Olivelle translations, and in annotated editions of Chanakya Niti.

Is “a man is great by deeds, not by birth” by Chanakya?

It is widely attributed to him, but we have not traced it to a securely identified source.
