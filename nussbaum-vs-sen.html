Short answer Both thinkers judge societies by what people are able to do and to be. What they share

Sen and Nussbaum both reject judging a society only by income, resources or utility. Both hold that two people with the same resources can have very different real freedom, and both treat the individual person as the unit of moral concern. They co-edited The Quality of Life (1993) and still build on each other's work. For the basics, start with our Capabilities Approach guide.

The dispute over the list

Nussbaum argues that a theory of justice needs a definite account of what citizens are owed. Without a list, she says, it becomes hard to criticize a society that defends deprivation by appeal to local culture or preference. A list also gives constitutions and courts something concrete to apply.

Sen replies that a fixed list is too rigid and risks putting a philosopher's judgment in place of democratic debate. In his view, which capabilities matter most depends on the purpose of the assessment and on what people can reason out together. Sen does not deny that some capabilities, such as being healthy or educated, are almost always important. He objects to a single, canonical list fixed once and for all.

Different kinds of theory

Nussbaum's approach is closer to John Rawls's tradition: it names basic entitlements and tries to ground them in an overlapping agreement among people with different worldviews. Sen's The Idea of Justice (2009) criticizes the search for a perfectly just society and favors comparative judgments: is this change an improvement on that arrangement? That makes his approach easier to apply to real policy choices and less ambitious about defining ideal justice.

The Nussbaum–Sen exchange

The disagreement is documented in a short exchange in Feminist Economics: Nussbaum's "Capabilities as Fundamental Entitlements: Sen and Social Justice" (2003) and Sen's reply, "Capabilities, Lists, and Public Reason: Continuing the Conversation" (2004). The tone is courteous. Both agree that the approach matters; they differ over how much philosophical specification it needs.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main difference between Nussbaum and Sen?

Nussbaum proposes a specific list of ten central capabilities as a basis for constitutional guarantees, while Sen declines to fix one list and favors public reasoning to decide which capabilities matter.

Did Nussbaum and Sen work together?

Yes. They co-edited The Quality of Life (1993) and have engaged each other's work for decades, while disagreeing on the list of capabilities.

Who developed the Capabilities Approach first?

Amartya Sen pioneered the capability framework; Nussbaum developed her own version from Aristotelian and liberal sources.

Which approach is used in development policy?

Sen's broader framework is more closely associated with the UNDP's Human Development Reports, while Nussbaum's list is influential in law, constitutional theory and philosophy.
