Stoicism Study Guide

A supplementary guide to Stoic history, key thinkers, concepts and practices, with a reading list and self-test questions. It is designed to be read alongside the full article on Stoicism.

Read PDF → Download PDF

What the Guide Covers

The Stoicism Study Guide provides a structured introduction to the history, major thinkers and philosophical principles of Stoicism. It brings together important ideas from different periods of the school and provides supporting material to help readers develop a clearer understanding of its philosophical foundations.

  • History of Stoicism: How the school originated in Athens, developed during the Hellenistic period and gained influence in the Roman world.
  • Major Stoic philosophers: The contributions of Zeno of Citium, Cleanthes, Chrysippus, Seneca, Epictetus and Marcus Aurelius.
  • Fundamental philosophical concepts: Virtue, wisdom, the dichotomy of control, impressions, assent and the relationship between Stoic ethics, logic and physics.
  • Stoic principles and practices: An introduction to how Stoic ideas concerning reason, nature, emotions and ethical living relate to human behaviour and everyday life.
  • Further reading: A selection of primary Stoic texts and introductory resources for readers who want to explore the philosophy in greater depth.
  • Self-test questions: Questions and discussion prompts designed to help readers review important concepts and assess their understanding.

The guide is intended as a companion to, rather than a replacement for, the full article on Stoicism. For a quick visual overview of its philosophical principles, see the Stoic concept map and the Stoicism timeline. These resources provide different ways of approaching the same philosophical tradition.

Who It Is For

This guide is written primarily for readers who are new to Stoicism and Hellenistic philosophy but want to develop a structured understanding of the subject. It may be useful to students taking introductory courses in philosophy, classics or ancient history, members of reading groups, and independent learners interested in exploring the philosophical foundations of Stoicism.

No previous knowledge of Greek or Roman philosophy is required. The guide introduces important thinkers, historical developments and philosophical concepts before encouraging readers to explore the original writings of Stoic philosophers. It can also serve as a revision resource for those who have already begun studying the subject.

Ways to Use the Guide

  • Before a seminar: Use it as introductory reading before a discussion on Stoic ethics, Hellenistic philosophy or ancient Greek and Roman thought.
  • For independent study: Work through individual sections to become familiar with important Stoic concepts and their historical background.
  • As a revision resource: Review the explanations and answer the self-test questions without referring to the material to check your understanding.
  • For reading groups: Use the guide as background material when studying the works of Epictetus, Seneca or Marcus Aurelius.
  • For further research: Use the reading list to move from introductory explanations towards primary philosophical texts and more advanced studies.
